# Tiff's Audience & Voice — Authoritative Positioning

**Consult this file before writing any class plan description or non-lifestyle newsletter.**

## Who TWY is for

People with an established yoga practice who want to deepen it.
Not beginners. Not peak-pose chasers.
Practitioners who want yoga to become part of how they live —
who want community, depth, and steady refinement.

## Who TWY is NOT for

- Absolute beginners ("never done yoga before")
- People looking for a fitness-style class
- Drop-in seekers without a foundational practice

The free Yoga Habit class is a **filter, not an entry point** —
designed to attract the right fit and let the wrong fit self-select out.

## Banned phrases (never use)

In class plan descriptions, non-lifestyle newsletters, landing copy:

- "all levels"
- "no experience needed" / "no experience required"
- "newer and experienced students" / "for both newer and seasoned"
- "accessible to everyone" / "accessible to all"
- "perfect for beginners" / "great for beginners"
- "welcoming to all"
- "any level"
- "every level"
- "open to all"
- "no commitment"

## Right framing

- "for people with an established practice who want to deepen it"
- "not a beginner class"
- "demystifies a commonly misunderstood pose"
- "for students ready to refine [pose]"
- "meets practitioners ready to deepen their understanding"
- "depth, refinement, established practice"
- "[free] on Zoom" — keep "free" but not "no commitment"

## Voice

Tiff: warm, specific, a little irreverent. Direct. Anusara-informed
without being dogmatic. Storytelling over instruction.
Not a philosophy essay. Not a marketing brochure.
"Here's what's happening this month" energy.

## Examples

WRONG: "This class offers options for both newer and experienced students."
RIGHT: "This class meets practitioners ready to deepen their understanding of Camel."

WRONG: "No experience needed. No membership. No commitment."
RIGHT: "For people with an established practice who want to deepen it. Not a beginner class. Free on Zoom."

WRONG: "Welcoming to all levels."
RIGHT: "Demystifies a commonly misunderstood pose for those serious about their practice."
